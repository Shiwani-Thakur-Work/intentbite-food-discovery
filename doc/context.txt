# AI Feature Rebuild: Natural-Language Food Discovery

## 1. Product context

Many food discovery experiences begin with restaurant names, cuisines, or conventional filters. A user may instead have a specific combination of needs: a fixed budget, dietary preferences, group size, delivery constraints, and a particular craving.

The proposed feature lets users express these needs in natural language and translates them into useful, explainable search results.

## 2. Problem statement

Users with multiple constraints may need to navigate several filters or repeatedly adjust their search to find suitable options.

The product opportunity is to make complex search intent easier to express and ensure the resulting options actually satisfy the stated constraints.

## 3. Target users

Start with users who regularly order food and need to balance multiple constraints, such as budget, dietary preferences, and meal type.

Do not assume that every food-ordering user experiences this problem equally.

## 4. Core hypothesis

If users can describe their requirements in one natural-language request and see results that clearly satisfy those requirements, they will find suitable options with less search effort.

## 5. Proposed MVP

- A search box for natural-language requests.
- Extraction of budget, dietary preferences, cuisine, and other supported constraints.
- A small, curated dataset of food or restaurant options.
- Results ranked against explicit constraints.
- A visible explanation of why each result matches.
- A way to correct misunderstood requirements.
- A fallback when no result satisfies every constraint.

For the prototype, use a curated dataset rather than depending on expensive or unreliable live restaurant APIs.

## 6. Product decisions

- Which constraints are mandatory and which are preferences?
- What happens when two constraints conflict?
- Should the system ask a clarifying question or return partial matches?
- How should users correct an incorrectly interpreted request?
- How will the product distinguish verified data from generated descriptions?

Hard constraints such as a maximum budget should not silently be violated just because a result appears otherwise relevant.

## 7. Success metrics

- Percentage of queries for which the system correctly interprets constraints.
- Percentage of results that satisfy all mandatory constraints.
- Time or number of interactions required to find a suitable option.
- Percentage of users who accept a result or refine the search.
- Rate of misunderstood constraints and empty-result failures.

## 8. Validation plan

First, observe how users currently search for meals under multiple constraints. Create representative test queries and manually define which results should qualify.

Compare the conventional filtering workflow with the natural-language prototype. Measure constraint satisfaction and task completion instead of relying only on subjective impressions.

## 9. Out of scope

- Full food delivery and checkout.
- Restaurant inventory integrations.
- Nationwide restaurant coverage.
- Personalized recommendations based on sensitive inferred attributes.
- A complete replacement for an existing food-ordering platform.

## 10. Portfolio outcome

Present a focused before-and-after workflow, explain why this feature was selected, show how intent extraction and ranking work, and report where the new experience succeeds or fails against the baseline.



- This idea is deliberately narrower than a full product. The goal is to identify one point of friction in an existing workflow and demonstrate how a thoughtful AI feature can improve it.
For a first experiment, I would consider natural-language food discovery: finding meals based on budget, dietary preferences, and practical constraints. This is an illustrative direction from your original idea, not a validated user problem.

- PM perspective: This is a good compact feature experiment. Keep it to a short, measurable case study rather than turning it into a sixth large application.
